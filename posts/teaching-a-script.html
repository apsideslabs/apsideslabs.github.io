Teaching a script, not just a language

Learning Assamese or Bodo means learning a script. What we changed after watching people try.

A Apsides Labs · 26 September 2026 · 5 min read

When people say they want to learn a language, they usually mean they want to speak it. For Assamese and Bodo, that quietly assumes something harder first: a script that is unfamiliar, and a keyboard that does not have it.

Watching people use our tools made it obvious that the script is not a preliminary. It is the subject.

The script is the first wall

A learner who cannot read a word cannot look it up, cannot sound it out, and cannot connect the written form to the spoken one. Vocabulary lists are useless until the letters mean something. So the script has to come first — and it has to be taught, not just displayed.

What we changed

  • Lead with the script. Letters and their sounds before words, so the learner can decode anything they meet.
  • Pair every letter with sound. A character with no audio is a shape to memorise. A character with audio is a sound you can hold.
  • Show it in context early. Real words as soon as a handful of letters are known, so the script stops being abstract.
  • Keep it offline. Practice happens in the gaps — on a bus, with no signal — so it has to work with no connection.

Small letters, many attempts

Script learning is repetition, and repetition is only bearable when each attempt is short. We broke practice into small rounds with immediate feedback rather than long lessons with a test at the end. People come back to a five-minute round; they avoid a forty-minute one.

What we learned: do not separate the script from the language. Teach the letters as the first real lesson, with sound and context attached, and the rest of the language becomes reachable.

Why it matters

Scripts are where low-resource languages lose learners. If the first experience is a wall of unfamiliar characters and no way to hear them, most people stop. If the first experience is a few letters, each with a sound, that you can use immediately — they keep going. That difference is the whole game.
